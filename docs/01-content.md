### Kathmandu Valley

_Central Nepal · Palaces, stupas and mountain sunrises_  
**SEO title:** Kathmandu Valley Tours: Temples, Stupas & Day Trips  
**Meta description:** Explore Kathmandu Durbar Square, Swayambhunath, Pashupatinath, Boudhanath and sunrise viewpoints like Nagarkot with Ashutosh Travels & Tours.

**Card summary:** Nepal's capital and its two sister cities, ringed by mountains and filled with palaces, stupas and temples. The valley is also the gateway to sunrise viewpoints and hill towns.

Kathmandu is the capital and largest metropolitan city of Nepal. It shares the Kathmandu Valley with two historic cities, Lalitpur (Patan) and Bhaktapur, and is surrounded by four major hills: Shivapuri, Phulchowki, Nagarjun and Chandragiri.

Nepali is the common language, and many residents also speak Newari, as the city is the centre of Newar people and culture. English is widely understood. Kathmandu has the most advanced infrastructure of any urban area in Nepal.

**Climate.** The valley enjoys a pleasant climate for most of the year. Between October and March, nights turn cold and the days are short, though mid-morning to afternoon is usually sunny and warm. Temperatures rise from April, often with afternoon storms.

#### Highlights in and around the valley

_In the valley_

- **Kathmandu Durbar Square** (UNESCO World Heritage, Palace). A complex of palaces, courtyards and temples built between the 12th and 18th centuries, once the seat of Kathmandu's Malla kings. Look for the 17th-century stone inscription set into the palace wall, written in 15 languages. The square remains the city's social, religious and urban heart, and the palace houses three museums.
- **Swayambhunath Stupa** (UNESCO World Heritage, Buddhist). Watching over the valley from a hilltop on its western side, this great stupa is one of Nepal's holiest Buddhist sites. Its origins are linked to the legend of the valley's creation from a primordial lake. Also known as Samhengu, it is a World Heritage Site, with the National Museum and the Museum of Natural History at the foot of the hill.
- **Pashupatinath Temple** (UNESCO World Heritage, Hindu). One of the most sacred Hindu shrines in the world, 5 km east of the city centre. The richly ornamented pagoda houses the sacred lingam of Lord Shiva, and chronicles record the temple before 400 AD. Devotees take ritual dips in the holy Bagmati River that flows beside it.
- **Boudhanath Stupa** (UNESCO World Heritage, Buddhist). About 6 km east of downtown Kathmandu, Boudhanath is the largest stupa in the valley, rising 36 metres and one of the finest examples of stupa design anywhere. It is also known as Khasti, or "dew drops", after the story that its builders mixed the mortar with dew during a severe drought.
- **Changu Narayan Temple** (UNESCO World Heritage, Hindu). Set on a ridge about 12 km east of the city and dedicated to Lord Vishnu, this is one of the finest and oldest examples of pagoda architecture, embellished with exquisite wood and stone carving.
- **Kirtipur** (Newar town). An ancient Newar township on a ridge 10 km southwest of Kathmandu, a natural fortress with a proud and courageous history. Wander quaint streets of artistic houses and temple squares, and visit the Chilamchu stupa and the temple of Bagh Bhairab. Kirtipur's people are known for their building and weaving skills.
- **Dakshinkali & Chobhar** (Hindu, Gorge). Dakshinkali, 22 km from the city on the valley's southern rim, is dedicated to the goddess Kali and is one of Kathmandu's most popular places of worship, busiest on Tuesdays and Saturdays when animal sacrifices are offered. On the way back, stop at Chobhar Gorge, the narrow cleft said to have been cut by Manjushree to drain the lake that once filled the valley.
- **Hattiban** (Nature, Outdoor). A 15 km drive from Kathmandu, Hattiban sits some 400 m above the valley among pine and fir forest. Enjoy pony rides, mountain biking, nature walks and hikes to Champa Devi Hill (2,054 m), or to Khokana and Bungamati for a taste of rural valley life.
_Beyond the valley_

- **Nagarkot** (Sunrise viewpoint, 2,175 m). The most popular mountain viewpoint near Kathmandu, 30 km to the east and a favourite weekend getaway. At sunrise, the Himalaya from Dhaulagiri in the west past Everest to Kanchenjunga in the east emerges from the darkness in breathtaking splendour.
- **Dhulikhel** (Newar town, 1,440 m). Just past Banepa, 32 km east of Kathmandu, this traditional Newar town is a wonderful place to spend the night and wake to sunrise over the Himalayan range. Once an important stop on the ancient trade route to Tibet, its glorious past lives on in handsome buildings and intricate woodcarving.
- **Balthali Village** (Countryside retreat). A quiet settlement on a plateau beyond Panauti, about 40 km from Kathmandu, where the Roshi and Ladku Khola rivers meet. Terraced rice fields and ochre thatched farmhouses frame clear views of Dorje Lakpa, Langtang and the wider Himalayan range on the northern horizon.
- **Bandipur** (Heritage hill town, 1,030 m). A beautifully preserved hilltop town in Tanahun District, 143 km west of Kathmandu and 80 km east of Pokhara. Once a simple Magar village, it grew into a prosperous trading centre with neoclassical facades, shuttered windows and slate-paved streets. Visit the Bindyabasini temple, Thani Mai, Tindhara and Raniban, trek down to Siddha Cave or hike to Ramkot village.
- **Tansen (Palpa)** (Hill town). A small, ancient hill town on the route between Pokhara and Lumbini, loved for its culture, serene atmosphere, friendly people and pleasant weather in every season. On clear days you can see Dhaulagiri, Annapurna, Manaslu, Gauri Shankar and more. ⚠️ _Verify: The PDF gives Tansen's altitude as 4,300 m, which is incorrect; altitude omitted until confirmed._
- **Kakani** (Mountain views, 1,982 m). A quieter, more pristine retreat 23 km northwest of Kathmandu, with close-up views of Ganesh Himal, Langtang and the Annapurnas, and oak and rhododendron forest on the slopes leading down to Balaju.
- **Daman** (Panoramic views, 2,400 m). For travellers seeking the ultimate Himalayan panorama, Daman is worth the extra distance. Some 80 km southwest of the valley on the highway towards the Indian border, it reveals the world's highest mountain range from horizon to horizon.
- **Gorkha** (History, 1,135 m). 131 km west of Kathmandu, reached by a road branching north from the Kathmandu–Pokhara highway at Abu Khaireni. The ancestral home of the Shah dynasty and the original Gorkha soldiers, it is crowned by Gorkha Durbar, a fort, palace and temple with superb views of the valleys and the Manaslu range.
- **Manakamana** (Pilgrimage, Cable car, 1,302 m). A hilltop temple in Gorkha dedicated to the wish-fulfilling goddess Manakamana ("mana" means heart, "kamana" means wish). A 10-minute cable car ride from Kurintar takes you to the top.

Patan, officially Lalitpur, is one of Nepal's major cities, in the southwest of the Kathmandu Valley. It is best known for its rich cultural heritage, especially its arts and crafts, and is called the city of festivals and feasts, fine ancient art, and metal and stone sculpture.

Temples of many sizes and styles fill the western side of Patan Durbar Square, chief among them the Krishna Temple, the Bhimsen Temple and the Golden Temple of Hiranya Varna.

#### Must-see sites in Patan

- **Krishna Temple** (Shikhara style, 17th century). This red-stone temple to Lord Krishna was built in the 17th century and is considered one of the jewels of Durbar Square. It is regarded as the first temple built in the shikhara style, which gives it a commanding presence, and it is often compared to sacred Mount Meru.
- **Golden Temple (Hiranya Varna Mahavihar)** (Buddhist monastery, 12th century). Known to Newars as Kwa Bahal, this unique Buddhist monastery was founded in the 12th century, with records from 1409. Its modest entrance gives no hint of the magnificent courtyard within, where stone gateways were made by artisans whose descendants still work in the woodcarving trade.
- **Kumbheshwar Temple** (Five-tiered pagoda, Shiva). A five-storey pagoda to Lord Shiva built by King Jayasthiti Malla, with golden embellishments added in 1422 AD. Its natural spring is said to be fed by the sacred glacial lake of Gosainkunda, and ritual bathing and a fair are held here during the Janai Purnima festival.
- **Jagatnarayan Temple** (Shikhara style, Vishnu). A tall red-brick shikhara temple dedicated to Lord Vishnu, with fine metal statues of Garuda, Ganesh and Hanuman.
- **Mahabouddha** (Buddhist, Terracotta). East of Durbar Square, this Buddhist temple of clay bricks is engraved with hundreds of Buddha images and is celebrated for its fine terracotta work.
- **Rudra Varna Mahavihar** (Buddhist monastery). A monastery with a remarkable collection of images and idols in metal, stone and wood. Legend says ancient kings were crowned here, and many treasures offered by devotees can still be seen.
- **The Ashokan Stupas** (3rd century BC). Emperor Ashoka is said to have visited in 250 BC and built four stupas at the four corners of Patan, in Pulchowk, Lagankhel, Ebahi and Teta (on the way to Sano Gaun).

### Bhaktapur

_Kathmandu Valley · The city of devotees_  
**SEO title:** Bhaktapur Durbar Square Tours: Nyatapola & the Golden Gate  
**Meta description:** Visit Bhaktapur's Golden Gate, Palace of Fifty-Five Windows and Nyatapola Temple, and taste its famous curd, with Ashutosh Travels & Tours.

**Card summary:** A showcase of Newari architecture, with pagoda squares, the Golden Gate and famous homemade curd, khopa dhau.

Bhaktapur, also known as Bhadgaon or the City of Devotees, is the third major town of the Kathmandu Valley. It was the capital of the whole valley from the 14th to the 16th century, and much of its great architecture dates from the late 17th century. Rising on the northern bank of the Hanumante River, it is renowned for Newari architecture.

Bhaktapur Durbar Square gathers pagoda and shikhara temples around the brick-and-wood Palace of Fifty-Five Windows. Statues of ancient kings on stone columns, guardian deities in their shrines, and woodcarving on every strut, lintel, gateway and window come together like a well-orchestrated symphony.

Don't leave without tasting Bhaktapur's famous homemade curd, known locally as khopa dhau, or browsing the square for handmade souvenirs such as woodcarvings, puppets, thangkas and caps. All foreign visitors pay an entry fee to Durbar Square.

#### Highlights of Bhaktapur

- **The Lion Gate** (1696 AD). A massive gate dating from 1696 AD, guarded on each side by huge stone lions, with imposing images of Lord Shiva and the goddess Ugrachandi.
- **The Golden Gate (Sun Dhoka)** (Metalwork masterpiece). Often called the most beautiful gate of its kind in the world, richly moulded with mythical creatures of marvellous intricacy. Erected by King Ranjit Malla, it leads into the main courtyard of the Palace of Fifty-Five Windows.
- **Palace of Fifty-Five Windows** (15th century, Woodcarving). Built during the reign of King Yaksha Malla in 1427 AD and remodelled by King Bhupatindra Malla in the 17th century. Its balcony of fifty-five windows is considered a unique masterpiece of woodcarving. ⚠️ _Verify: The PDF's text for this site mixes in a description of Patan's palace (Keshav Narayan Chowk); only the parts that refer to Bhaktapur are kept._
- **The Art Gallery** (Museum). Ancient paintings from Hindu and Buddhist traditions across many periods, especially valued by researchers of both faiths.
- **Statue of King Bhupatindra Malla** (Sculpture). Atop a column facing the palace, the king is shown in an attitude of worship. It is considered the most magnificent of the square's many statues.
- **Nyatapola Temple** (1702 AD, Five-tiered pagoda). Nepal's most famous pagoda, built in 1702 AD by King Bhupatindra Malla and dedicated to Siddhilaxmi, the tantric mother goddess. Its name means "five-tiered" in Newari, symbolising the five elements: water, earth, fire, wind and sky.
- **Pashupati Temple** (Hindu, Folklore). Folklore tells of a Bhaktapur devotee of Lord Pashupati who dreamed that Shiva asked for a temple to live in. He obeyed, and the temple was built.

**Climate.** Pokhara's climate is subtropical, but its elevation keeps temperatures moderate: summer averages 25–35°C and winter around 5–15°C.

#### Things to see in Pokhara

- **Phewa Lake** (Boating, Mountain reflections). The second-largest lake in Nepal, roughly 1.5 km by 4 km, and the centre of Pokhara life. Visit the pagoda-style Barahi temple to the water goddess on its island, and admire Machhapuchhre and Annapurna mirrored in the water; the south-eastern shore has the best views. The lake is up to about 47 m deep and its water is warm.
- **Begnas & Rupa Lakes** (Peaceful retreat, Fishing). About 13–15 km from Pokhara, at the end of a road that turns north off the Kathmandu highway. Begnas is the valley's second-largest lake, and the pair's relative isolation makes them a perfect nature retreat for boating and fishing.
- **World Peace Pagoda** (Viewpoint, Buddhist). A massive Buddhist stupa on a hilltop above the southern shore of Phewa Lake. A short hike brings you to spectacular views of the Annapurna range and the city.
- **Davis Falls (Patale Chhango)** (Waterfall). One of Pokhara's best-known sights, fed by Phewa Lake. A modern legend tells of a foreigner named David swept into the underground passage beneath the falls when the dam's floodgates opened, never to be seen again.
- **Mahendra Cave** (Limestone cave). Known locally as Chamero Odhaar, the "House of Bats". Discovered by shepherd boys around 1950 and named after the late King Mahendra, the large limestone cave is lit for visitors and known for rocks that glitter in the light.

### Chitwan

_Inner Terai, South-Central Nepal · Rhinos, tigers and the wild Terai_  
**SEO title:** Chitwan National Park Safari: Rhinos, Tigers & Jungle Activities  
**Meta description:** Jungle walks, 4WD drives, canoe rides and bird watching in Chitwan National Park, home of the one-horned rhino and the Bengal tiger.

**Card summary:** Nepal's oldest national park, home to the one-horned rhino and the Bengal tiger, with jungle walks, canoe rides and Tharu culture.

Chitwan is one of the few remaining undisturbed corners of the Terai, the lowland belt that once stretched along the foothills of India and Nepal. Its flora and fauna are exceptionally rich.

The park shelters one of the last populations of the one-horned Asian rhinoceros and is one of the last refuges of the Bengal tiger. It is a great place to spot the Bengal florican, giant hornbill and grass owl, while gharial thrive in the Narayani River and mugger crocodiles live in its many lakes.

**Climate.** Chitwan has a humid tropical monsoon climate. November to February is generally dry and cool, warming by the end of March. June to September is hot and very humid, with the monsoon arriving mid-June and lasting until mid-September.

#### Wildlife of Chitwan National Park

- **Large mammals**. Elephants and Indian rhinos are the largest residents, joined by gaur, sambar, Indian muntjac, chital, hog deer, mainland serow, chousingha and wild boar.
- **Predators**. Tigers, leopards and dholes hunt here, while striped hyenas scavenge. Sloth bears are among the park's star attractions.
- **Smaller carnivores**. Golden jackal, yellow-throated marten, ratel, smooth-coated otter, small and large Indian civets, spotted linsang, common palm civet, binturong, several mongoose species, and leopard, marbled and fishing cats.
- **And more**. Rhesus monkeys, Hanuman langurs, Indian pangolin, flying squirrels, Indian porcupine, hispid hare and the Ganges river dolphin.

### Lumbini

_Terai, southern Nepal · Birthplace of Lord Buddha_  
**SEO title:** Lumbini Pilgrimage Tours: Birthplace of Lord Buddha  
**Meta description:** Visit the Sacred Garden, Mayadevi Temple and Ashoka Pillar at Lumbini, the UNESCO-listed birthplace of the Buddha, with Ashutosh Travels & Tours.

**Card summary:** The sacred garden where Siddhartha Gautama was born, marked by Emperor Ashoka's pillar and a place of pilgrimage for the world.

Lumbini, in the Terai plains of southern Nepal, is where the Buddha was born in 623 BC. The site is marked by a stone pillar erected by Emperor Ashoka in 249 BC, inscribed to identify it as the birthplace.

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, Lumbini is being developed with international support as a supreme Buddhist pilgrimage destination and a symbol of world peace. The Sacred Garden centres on the Ashoka Pillar, beside the renovated Mayadevi Temple and the Puskarini pond where Queen Mayadevi bathed before giving birth.

**Climate.** Lumbini has a tropical monsoon climate with cold, hot, monsoon and transitional seasons. The coldest period runs from early December to the end of January, when temperatures can fall to around 6°C. The weather is pleasant most of the year.

#### Sacred places to visit

- **Sacred Garden**. The birthplace of Prince Siddhartha, with the Ashokan pillar whose inscription identifies the spot as the Buddha's birthplace.
- **Mayadevi Temple**. Dedicated to Maya Devi, the Buddha's mother, and believed to stand over one of Ashoka's original stupas. Panels depicting the holy birth were installed by Malla kings who ruled the Karnali region from the 11th to the 15th century. The site was rediscovered by a German archaeologist in 1895.
- **Puskarini Pond**. The sacred pool beside the Mayadevi Temple where Queen Mahamaya is believed to have bathed before giving birth, and where the newborn Siddhartha was first bathed.
- **Sanctum of the Birthplace**. Uncovered by careful excavation beneath the ruins of the old Mayadevi temple, a single stone slab deep in the sanctum marks the exact spot of the Buddha's birth.

### White-water rafting

White-water rafting means riding a rushing river in an inflatable raft or kayak, over crashing waves and through swirling rapids, for the excitement of a lifetime. Nepal has earned a reputation as one of the world's best rafting destinations.

Rivers are graded on a scale of one to six. Many travellers combine a Trisuli run with a trip to Pokhara or Chitwan National Park.

- **Trisuli River.** Nepal's most popular river for short trips, with easy access from the Prithvi Highway that runs along its banks. Ideal for beginners, and even bigger during the monsoon.
- **Bhote Koshi River.** One of the best short trips in Nepal: about 18 km of continuous white water and some of the most action-packed paddling in the country, flowing down from the Tibet border.
- **Kali Gandaki River.** Through remote canyons in the world's deepest gorge, with bone-chilling rapids and outstanding views of snow-capped peaks. The river later joins the Narayani.
- **Arun River.** Reach Tumlingtar by plane, or by a day's drive and three days' trek. Among Nepal's most challenging rafting, with remote countryside and spectacular mountain views.
- **Seti River.** A warm, blue river rising on Mardi Himal beside Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), flowing partly underground through the Pokhara Valley. Limestone gorges, green forest and crystal-clear rapids make it ideal for learning to kayak.
- **Marsyangdi River.** Its name means "raging river" in the local dialect, and it is one of the world's best white-water runs. Trek through Gurung villages and emerald fields to Ngadi, then paddle beneath colossal Himalayan peaks. The first 90 km are steep and technical; in the final 90 km, scenery and wildlife take centre stage.

**River grades**

- **Grade I (Easy):** Moving water with occasional small rapids and few or no obstacles.
- **Grade II (Moderate):** Small rapids with regular waves. Some manoeuvring needed, but easy to navigate.
- **Grade III (Difficult):** Rapids with irregular waves and hazards. More difficult manoeuvring, though routes are usually obvious. Occasional scouting from the shore.
- **Grade IV (Very difficult):** Large rapids needing careful manoeuvring around dangerous hazards. Scouting is often necessary and rescue is usually difficult. Turbulent water and big, irregular waves may flip rafts.
- **Grade V (Extremely difficult):** Long, very violent rapids with severe hazards. Continuous, powerful water can make route-finding difficult.
- **Grade VI (Nearly impossible):** The extreme limit of navigability. Possibly runnable by expert teams at the right water level, in the right conditions, with every precaution, but still with considerable risk to life.

## Nepal guide

### Nepal: the roof of the world, and so much more

Nepal is known for the highest Himalayan peaks, artistic monuments, exotic wildlife and a remarkably diverse culture. Set between China and India, it is a largely agricultural country home to many ethnic groups, each with its own language and traditions, and a people who live by the saying "Unity in Diversity".

Few places on earth offer so much variety in one journey. Nepal is a world-class destination for hiking, trekking, rock climbing, peak climbing and mountaineering; for kayaking, rafting and canoeing; for wildlife safaris, mountain biking and paragliding; and for cultural sites at every turn. It is also the land where Lord Buddha was born more than 2,500 years ago.

**A brief history**

Nepal was unified in 1768 by King Prithvi Narayan Shah. Under Shah and his successors, its borders reached as far west as Kashmir and as far east as Sikkim (now part of India).

A commercial treaty was signed with Britain in 1792, and again in 1816 after more than a year of hostilities with the British East India Company.

### National flag

Nepal's flag is the only national flag in the world that is not rectangular. Its design draws on an original Hindu form used for more than 2,000 years.

It is crimson red with a blue border, and carries two emblems: a crescent moon at the top and the sun at the bottom.

### Language

Nepali is the official language, alongside around 20 other languages and numerous dialects. Derived from Sanskrit and related to Hindi, Nepali is spoken by about 90% of the population as a first or second language.

Most people in Kathmandu and other large cities understand and speak English.

### Religion

Hinduism is the major religion, and Nepal is also home to Buddhist, Muslim and Christian communities. All faiths live together in notable harmony.

### Time zone

Nepal Standard Time is GMT +5:45, which is 15 minutes ahead of Indian Standard Time.

### Currency

The official currency is the Nepalese Rupee (NPR), divided into 100 paisa. Banknotes are issued in a range of denominations up to 1,000 rupees.

### Weekly holiday

Saturday is the official weekly holiday, and most shops close for the day. Museums close on Tuesdays and on government holidays.

### Climate

Nepal's climate ranges from subtropical summers and mild winters in the southern lowlands to an alpine climate, with cool summers and severe winters, in the mountains.

Average annual rainfall falls from 1,778 mm (70 in) in the east to 899 mm (35 in) in the west.

### Vegetation

For its size, Nepal has some of the most outstanding biodiversity in the world, from subtropical rainforest to alpine desert.

### Customs

Duty-free: 200 cigarettes, 50 cigars, 1.15 litres of distilled spirits. ⚠️ _Verify: Allowances are from the PDF (which also allowed 15 rolls of film). Confirm current limits with Nepal Customs._

You may bring the following items duty-free, provided you take them with you when you leave: binoculars, a video camera, a still camera, a laptop computer and a portable music system.
